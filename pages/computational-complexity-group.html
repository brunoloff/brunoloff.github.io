Our purpose is to create and maintain at DCC a group of people who are able to study and understand the state-of-the-art work in Computational Complexity.

We are interested in teaching, learning and generally promoting the following topics, with anyone who is interested in them:

  • How to read and understand mathematical proofs.
  • Computational models: decision-trees, communication protocols, circuits, RAMs, data structure schemes, Turing machines, etc.
  • Algorithms, especially those that prove something surprising about complexity (Edmond’s algorithm, the ellipsoid method, FFT, fast matrix multiplication, Immerman–Szelepcsényi, etc).
  • Completeness notions: NP-completeness and completeness for other complexity classes, PCPs, etc.
  • All areas with applications to complexity theory, such as linear algebra, combinatorics, analysis of Boolean functions, etc

Above all, we are interested in lower-bounds: how does one show that an algorithm needs more than a certain amount of a certain resource (time, space, communication, etc), in order to solve a given problem?
